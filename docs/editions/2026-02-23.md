# Infectious Disease Epidemiology Brief

**Edition date:** February 23, 2026  
**Time window covered:** February 13–23, 2026 *(items older than this window are labeled as such)*

## 1) Executive Summary (5–8 bullets)

- **Measles (California; operational priority):** California measles activity remains the top acute operational risk, with a statewide count of **19 confirmed cases as of Feb 16** and guidance emphasizing **front-door screening** and **immediate airborne precautions** for suspected cases. *(Source: San Diego County CAHAN; link pending)*
- **Measles—Shasta County focal point:** Shasta County reported **9 total measles cases** (Feb 19 update) with multiple exposure sites requiring rapid contact tracing and isolation. [1]
- **Measles—Southern California exposure management:** Los Angeles County reported a **4th 2026 case** with time-specific exposure windows at **LAX Terminal B (Feb 9)** and multiple public locations; CDC is collaborating on international flight contact notifications. [2]
- **Respiratory season burden (influenza-led):** Seasonal respiratory viruses are driving healthcare utilization, with San Diego County reporting (through Feb 14) influenza ED diagnosis ~**5.5%**, **307 influenza hospitalizations** (week 6), and **1,867 influenza hospitalizations season-to-date**. [3]
- **Influenza strain dynamics (watch):** Signals suggest influenza strain dynamics worth tracking, including circulation of influenza A(H3N2) **subclade K** noted in local advisories and national characterization data. *(Local advisory is older than the window.)* [4]
- **High-impact “foodborne exposure” event (non-infectious):** SF Bay Area advisory (Feb 17) reports **severe amatoxin mushroom poisonings** reached **40 cases statewide** (Nov 18–Feb 9) with **4 deaths** and **3 liver transplants**, with risk to pets noted—functions operationally like a high-impact foodborne event requiring coordinated risk communication and clinical readiness. *(Link pending)*
- **National measles context (importation risk):** The U.S. reported **982 confirmed measles cases as of Feb 19** with **89% outbreak-associated**, increasing importation risk into travel hubs and mass gatherings. [5]
- **Preparedness implication (this week):** Treat **measles + influenza** as dual “front door” priorities—tighten triage/isolation workflows and vaccination/antiviral execution while maintaining occupational protections in healthcare and high-exposure workplaces.

## 2) California: Key Developments

### a) Respiratory viruses (influenza, RSV, SARS-CoV-2)

**What happened (signals of high clinical burden, influenza-led):**
- **San Diego County** (data through Feb 14) reported **2,247 influenza cases** in week 6 (season-to-date **16,453**), **307 influenza hospitalizations** in week 6 (season-to-date **1,867**), and ED visits with an influenza diagnosis ~**5.5%** (week 6). [3]
- **San Mateo County** (week Feb 8–14) reported decreases in influenza test positivity, ED visits, and influenza A wastewater compared with the prior week, while noting **influenza B wastewater increased to the highest levels this season**; estimated **42%** of residents had received 2025–26 influenza vaccine as of Feb 19. [6]
- **Influenza strain/subtyping note (older than window; Jan 12):** Alameda County advisory content highlighted increasing influenza and hospital admissions in CA and referenced influenza A(H3N2) **subclade K** circulating globally and in California. [4]

**Where:** San Diego County; San Mateo County; Alameda County advisory applies countywide (and City of Berkeley) for healthcare masking and respiratory season operations. [3]

**Who is affected:** Greatest near-term impact is on older adults, high-risk patients, and congregate settings (SNFs/residential outbreaks tracked in San Diego). [3]

**What we know vs. don’t know:**
- We have strong county-level syndromic and hospitalization signals (San Diego; San Mateo). [3], [6]
- Statewide comparability may be limited by reporting differences and lags (see Data Notes). [3]

**Public health response & guidance (including occupational elements):**
- **Alameda County workplace guidance** reminds employers that Cal/OSHA **non-emergency COVID regulations expired Feb 3, 2025**, but **COVID case recordkeeping remained required through Feb 3, 2026** (records retained until **Feb 3, 2028**); also emphasizes maintaining an effective **IIPP** and aligning with CDC respiratory virus prevention guidance. [7]
- Alameda County/City of Berkeley health leadership strongly recommends **high-quality masking** for staff/visitors in patient-care areas during winter respiratory season (**Nov 1–Mar 31**). [4]

**Practical implications for LHJs and clinicians (this week):**
- Re-check ED triage + testing pathways and ensure same-day antiviral initiation for high-risk influenza patients (consistent with local advisory messaging). [4]
- Use wastewater/syndromic signals (e.g., influenza B rise) to anticipate school/workplace absenteeism and staffing stress. [6]
- Reinforce facility masking norms during high circulation to protect vulnerable patients and sustain workforce capacity. [4]

### b) Vaccine-preventable diseases (measles, pertussis)

#### Measles (primary CA operational issue)

- **Statewide:** San Diego County CAHAN update reported **19 confirmed measles cases** in California as of Feb 16 and emphasizes point-of-entry screening and immediate airborne precautions for suspected measles to prevent healthcare-associated exposures. *(Link pending)*
- **Shasta County:** **9 total measles cases** reported Feb 19, with exposure sites including a school and a healthcare/urgent care setting—requiring rapid outreach and post-exposure management. [1]
- **Los Angeles County:** Feb 19 press release documents a **4th measles case** in 2026, with a clear exposure window at **LAX Terminal B on Feb 9** and additional community exposure locations; CDC collaborates on international flight contact notifications. [2]
- **Orange County:** Feb 7 health alert reports an infectious measles case visited **Disneyland Park** and **Disney California Adventure** on **Jan 22**; symptom window expected **7–21 days** post-exposure. *(Older than the window.)* [8]
- **Riverside County (Feb 9; older than window but central to the CA cluster narrative):** Child case without international or out-of-state travel, underscoring potential for local transmission once regional activity rises. [9]
- **San Bernardino County (Feb 6; older than window):** First confirmed case since 2023; exposure at an Ontario Walmart (Jan 29). *(Link pending)*

**What we know vs. don’t know (measles):**
- We know exposure settings (airports, theme parks, schools, healthcare) and that these can generate secondary cases if vaccination gaps exist. [2]
- We often do *not* know full transmission chains early; expect reporting lag and under-ascertainment in mild/atypical presentations.

**Clinical & public health operations (measles):**
- San Diego CAHAN guidance notes NP/OP swab for PCR is sufficient to diagnose acute measles (urine may improve sensitivity but is not required in that advisory), and reiterates airborne precautions as immediate action. *(Link pending)*

#### Pertussis (watch for concurrent pressure on pediatric care)

- **San Bernardino County (Jan 8; older than window):** Reminder that whooping cough is increasing and calls for keeping DTaP/Tdap up to date—relevant given simultaneous measles activity and seasonal respiratory burden. [10]

### c) Foodborne / waterborne

#### Severe amatoxin mushroom poisonings (California; high significance, partly outside window)

- **SF Bay Area advisory (Feb 17):** **40 cases statewide** (Nov 18–Feb 9), **4 deaths**, **3 liver transplants**; advisory notes risk to pets. *(Non-infectious, but operationally resembles a high-impact foodborne exposure event.)* *(Link pending)*
- **Sacramento County (Jan 22; older than window):** Confirmed **4 local cases** and reiterated statewide risk messaging to avoid foraging. [11]

#### Multi-state events likely to affect California supply chains/consumers

- **Multidrug-resistant Salmonella Newport—Rosabella moringa powder capsules:** CDC update (Feb 13) reports **7 cases across 7 states** with **3 hospitalizations**. [12]
- **Listeria outbreak—FreshRealm chicken fettuccine alfredo meals:** CDC update (Feb 12) reports meals contaminated with *Listeria monocytogenes* (investigation ongoing). [13]

**Practical implications:**
- Ensure local environmental health and clinical partners have fast pathways for product identification, specimen collection, and public messaging aligned with CDC outbreak notices. [12]

### d) Vector-borne / zoonotic

#### Leptospirosis (Berkeley animals; occupational/community interface)

- **Alameda County advisory (Jan 14; older than window):** Leptospirosis detected in rats and at least 2 dogs near a defined Berkeley location (Nov–Dec 2025), with **no associated human cases identified to date**; highlights risk for persons experiencing homelessness and those exposed to contaminated water/soil, and provides provider testing/treatment pointers (including considering antibiotics when suspicion is high). [4]

#### Avian influenza A(H5N1) (occupational risk emphasis)

- **CDPH guidance for health professionals:** General public risk remains low, but job-related or close/prolonged exposure to infected birds/cows/animals increases risk—supports targeted PPE and surveillance in high-exposure workplaces. [14]

#### Mpox (situational awareness)

- **CDPH mpox dashboard:** Data refresh noted Feb 13, 2026; supports ongoing situational monitoring (particularly for event-linked risk periods). [15]

## 5) Data Notes & Caveats

- **County respiratory reporting lags:** County respiratory reports may be revised; San Diego explicitly notes data are preliminary and may change with delayed reporting and investigation updates. [3]
- **Measles reporting cadence:** Measles totals vary by reporting schedule; CDC updates based on confirmed cases notified to CDC on a defined cadence, and jurisdictions may post faster local updates. [5]
- **Wastewater interpretation:** Wastewater trends can be directional “early signals” and should be interpreted alongside ED visits, hospitalizations, and lab testing (e.g., influenza B signal in San Mateo). [6]
- **Evidence grading:** Preprints and perspectives are included as interpretive context; prioritize jurisdictional advisories and surveillance for operational decisions.

---

## 6) One-Minute Takeaways (Actionable this week)

- **Run a measles “front door” drill:** fever+rash screening at entry, immediate masking/airborne isolation, and clear internal routing for PCR testing and public health notification.
- **Target MMR catch-up now** in schools, childcare, and among travelers—especially in counties with known exposures (Shasta/LA/OC/SB/Riverside). [1]
- **Influenza execution over influenza intention:** ensure antivirals are started rapidly for high-risk patients and confirm facilities have a current testing algorithm (outpatient molecular, inpatient PCR). [4]
- **Protect the healthcare workforce** during peak respiratory season with consistent facility masking norms and reinforced sick-leave/return-to-work expectations. [4]
- **Pre-brief outbreak response partners** on current CDC foodborne investigations (MDR Salmonella; Listeria prepared meals) so retail exposure questions can be handled quickly and consistently. [12]
