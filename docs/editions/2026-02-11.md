# Infectious Disease Epidemiology Brief

**Edition date:** February 11, 2026 (PT)  
**Time window covered:** February 1–11, 2026 *(items outside this window are labeled as such)*

---

## 1) Executive Summary (5–8 bullets)

- **Measles (California):** CDPH reports **17 confirmed measles cases** as of **Feb 9**, including an **8-case related cluster in Shasta County** and additional cases reported by multiple Southern California local health departments. [CDPH press release][1]
- **Influenza (California):** CDPH reports **high seasonal influenza activity** in many regions, with **increasing ED visits and hospitalizations**, especially among children. [CDPH Respiratory Virus Report][2]
- **County signal — Los Angeles (older than core window):** For week ending **Jan 24**, LA County reported **17.1% influenza test positivity**, increased influenza-like illness ED visits, and rising flu hospitalizations. [LA County DPH press release][3]
- **County signal — San Diego:** Season-to-date through **Jan 31** shows substantial respiratory burden across pathogens, including **influenza (11,429 cases; 1,236 hospitalizations)** and **COVID‑19 (2,044 hospitalizations)**, plus congregate-setting outbreaks.
- **RSV (California):** CDPH reports **increasing RSV activity** with hospitalization impacts in **children and older adults**; San Diego’s latest week shows ongoing RSV transmission. [CDPH Respiratory Virus Report][2]
- **Zoonotic/occupational — H5N1:** CDPH reiterates clinicians should consider **avian influenza A(H5N1)** in symptomatic people with relevant exposures (including conjunctivitis in California dairy workers) and urges rapid reporting; Cal/OSHA provides worker protection guidance (respiratory/eye protection, employer controls). [CDPH H5N1 clinical guidance][4]; [Cal/OSHA worker guidance][10]
- **National:** CDC reports influenza activity remains **elevated nationally** (FluView Week 4). [CDC FluView Week 4][5]
- **Preparedness implication:** Prioritize **MMR catch-up** and exposure management, and reinforce respiratory-season surge protocols (testing, antivirals, isolation/return-to-work) across healthcare, schools, and congregate settings. [CDPH Respiratory Virus Report][2]

## 2) California: Key Developments

### a) Respiratory viruses (influenza, RSV, SARS‑CoV‑2)

**What happened (statewide):** CDPH reports high influenza activity in many regions, increasing ED visits and hospitalizations, and increasing RSV activity; COVID‑19 activity remains very low statewide. [CDPH Respiratory Virus Report][2]

**Where:** Statewide (CDPH narrative), with strong local signals from San Diego and Los Angeles County. [CDPH Respiratory Virus Report][2]

**Who is affected:** CDPH highlights children (influenza positivity and increasing pediatric utilization); RSV impacts children and older adults. [CDPH Respiratory Virus Report][2]

**Local numeric context (San Diego County; through Jan 31):**
- **Week 4:** Influenza **1,937 cases**; RSV **204**; COVID‑19 **102**  
  - Hospitalizations: influenza **208**; RSV **31**; COVID‑19 **17**
- **Season-to-date:** Influenza **11,429 cases / 1,236 hospitalizations**; COVID‑19 **2,044 hospitalizations**; RSV **1,395 cases / 274 hospitalizations**
- **Congregate setting outbreaks (season-to-date):** COVID‑19 **102**, influenza **6**, RSV **2** (residential congregate settings definition)

**What we know vs. don’t know:**
- We do have CDPH’s statewide qualitative assessment and defined thresholds for activity/severity. [CDPH Respiratory Virus Report][2]
- We do not include a single statewide numeric snapshot here (county reports provide the clearest quantified signals in this briefing format).

**Public health response & guidance:**
- CDPH urges vaccination, testing, and rapid treatment to reduce severe outcomes; recommends respiratory virus immunizations for eligible people. [CDPH Respiratory Virus Report][2]
- CDPH reiterates outbreak and case reporting expectations for local health jurisdictions (LHJs) to support timely situational awareness. [CDPH LHJ reporting guidance][6]

**Practical implications (LHDs, clinicians, hospital epi, employers):**
- **Hospitals/LTCFs:** Stress-test surge plans (triage, cohorting, visitor policies) and ensure rapid test-to-treat pathways for influenza (antivirals) and COVID‑19 (when indicated). [CDPH Respiratory Virus Report][2]
- **Occupational health:** Review return-to-work and respiratory protection readiness (fit testing, PPE supply); monitor unit-level absenteeism and cluster signals. [CDPH Respiratory Virus Report][2]
- **Schools/childcare:** Reinforce vaccination messaging and exclusion policies for febrile respiratory illness; anticipate pediatric demand. [CDPH Respiratory Virus Report][2]

### b) Vaccine-preventable diseases (measles, pertussis, etc.)

**What happened:** CDPH reports **17 confirmed measles cases** as of **Feb 9, 2026** and issued a statewide communication urging Californians to ensure they are fully vaccinated, citing transmission in multiple counties and spread elsewhere in the U.S. [CDPH press release][1]

**Where:** Recent cases reported by local health departments in **Los Angeles, Orange, San Bernardino, Riverside, and Shasta** counties, with Shasta County reporting an **8-case related cluster**. [CDPH press release][1]

**Who is affected:** CDPH emphasizes vaccination status as the key risk factor; local clusters often concentrate among unvaccinated/under-vaccinated networks. [CDPH press release][1]

**What we know vs. don’t know:**
- **Known:** Case counts (statewide total and Shasta cluster) and multi-county distribution as of Feb 9–10. [CDPH press release][1]
- **Unknown/under investigation:** Additional suspected cases and full exposure networks across counties; investigations are ongoing. [CDPH press release][1]

**Public health response & guidance:**
- CDPH urges residents to check immunization status and obtain MMR vaccination as appropriate, particularly amid ongoing transmission and travel-related risk. [CDPH press release][1]

**Practical implications:**
- **Healthcare systems:** Verify MMR immunity among patient-facing staff and tighten airborne isolation readiness for febrile rash illnesses. [CDPH press release][1]
- **LHDs:** Prioritize rapid case investigation, PEP recommendations per existing protocols, and targeted outreach to low-coverage pockets. [CDC measles data and outbreaks][7]

### c) Foodborne/waterborne (Salmonella, *E. coli*, hepatitis A)

**What happened:** CDC expanded a multistate investigation of **Salmonella linked to dietary supplements containing moringa leaf powder**. As of **Jan 29**, CDC reported **65 illnesses across 28 states**, added an additional Salmonella strain, and updated recall information. [CDC Newsroom release][8]

**Where:** Multistate exposure via nationally distributed supplement products (potential California spillover via retail/online purchase). [CDC Newsroom release][8]

**Who is affected:** Higher risk for severe illness in young children, older adults, and immunocompromised people (CDC reiteration). [CDC Newsroom release][8]

**What we know vs. don’t know:**
- **Known:** Product category/vehicle (moringa-containing supplements), recalls, and case count reported by CDC as of Jan 29. [CDC Newsroom release][8]
- **Unknown:** California-specific case counts were not enumerated in CDC’s media alert; local burden may lag due to reporting and subtyping timelines. [CDC Newsroom release][8]

**Practical implications:**
- **Clinicians/labs:** Consider targeted exposure history for diarrheal illness (including supplement use); ensure specimens are cultured/subtyped when indicated. [CDC Newsroom release][8]
- **Retail/occupational:** Reinforce recall execution and sanitation of surfaces potentially contaminated by supplement powders/capsules. [CDC Newsroom release][8]

---

### d) Vector-borne/zoonotic (West Nile, mpox, rabies-related alerts, H5N1)

**Rabies (Los Angeles County):** LA County’s 2026 rabies map highlights rabid bat detections and emphasizes that areas without prior detections can still have rabid bats; guidance stresses reporting/testing and keeping pets vaccinated (page updated Feb 5). [LA County Rabies Map 2026][9]

**H5N1 (occupational/zoonotic):**
- CDPH advises considering avian influenza A(H5N1) in patients with acute respiratory symptoms and/or conjunctivitis plus recent relevant exposures; recommends prompt reporting and provides antiviral treatment/prophylaxis guidance. [CDPH H5N1 clinical guidance][4]
- Cal/OSHA provides worker-focused controls when bird flu is detected in animals, reinforcing employer responsibilities for protection (including respiratory/eye protection). [Cal/OSHA worker guidance][10]

**Practical implications:**
- **LHDs/clinicians:** Maintain a low threshold for consultation/testing pathways for exposed workers; ensure specimen collection includes conjunctival sampling when clinically indicated. [CDPH H5N1 clinical guidance][4]
- **Employers (dairy/poultry/animal handling):** Review PPE, training, and incident reporting; align occupational surveillance with public health reporting. [Cal/OSHA worker guidance][10]

---

### e) Healthcare-associated infections (HAIs) & antimicrobial resistance (AMR)

**What happened (respiratory HAIs/outbreak proxy):** San Diego reports substantial residential congregate-setting outbreaks season-to-date (**COVID‑19 102; influenza 6; RSV 2**), reinforcing that cluster/outbreak risk remains high during respiratory season.

**What we know vs. don’t know:**
- This briefing does not include a new statewide CDPH HAI/AR dashboard update within the past ~10 days; county outbreak surveillance indicates continued congregate-setting vulnerability.

**Practical implications:**
- **Hospitals/SNFs:** Re-check outbreak thresholds, testing cadence during clusters, and staff exclusion policies; prioritize vaccination plus ventilation/respiratory protection during peaks. [CDPH Respiratory Virus Report][2]

## 5) Data Notes & Caveats

- **Reporting lags:** Deaths and some hospitalization/outbreak tallies (especially recent weeks) can lag due to processing and verification; county and state dashboards may revise prior weeks.
- **Syndromic vs. confirmed:** ED syndromic metrics capture broad respiratory illness and can rise ahead of lab-confirmed increases. [CDC Respiratory Virus Activity Levels][11]
- **Comparability:** CDPH notes dashboard updates most Fridays and uses defined thresholds for activity/severity levels; cross-county comparisons should account for testing practices and healthcare-seeking behavior. [CDPH Respiratory Virus Report][2]
