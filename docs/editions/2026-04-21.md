# Infectious Disease Epidemiology Brief

**Edition date:** April 21, 2026  
**Coverage window:** April 11–21, 2026, with a few older California items included because they remain operationally important.

## 1) Executive Summary

California measles remains the highest-priority vaccine-preventable threat. CDPH reported 39 confirmed measles cases statewide as of April 13; within the last week, San Francisco confirmed its first measles case since 2019 in an unvaccinated infant exposed during international travel, while Sacramento County’s early-April alert highlighted repeated healthcare exposures before diagnosis. (California Department of Public Health)

Mpox activity is rising in California, with a new Bay Area signal. CDPH urged vaccination for higher-risk Californians as cases rise statewide, and San Francisco confirmed its first clade I mpox case in an unvaccinated adult who was hospitalized and is improving. (California Department of Public Health)

Respiratory pressure is lower overall, but RSV season has effectively been extended for infants. CDPH says combined influenza/RSV/COVID-19 healthcare-seeking burden is low, but it extended RSV monoclonal antibody use for eligible infants and some young children through April 30 because RSV activity is persisting. (California Department of Public Health)

A California-linked raw dairy outbreak remains an actionable food-safety issue. CDC’s multistate investigation tied to Raw Farm products remains open, with 9 cases, 3 hospitalizations, and 1 HUS case; CDPH has warned Californians not to consume recalled raw cheddar products. (CDC)

Avian influenza remains a low public-risk but ongoing occupational issue in California. CDPH continues to state that public risk is low and that no person-to-person spread has been detected in California, but workers exposed to dairy cattle, poultry, or wildlife remain at higher risk; CDPH also issued a recent warning about H5N1 in elephant seal pups at Año Nuevo. (California Department of Public Health)

Nationally, measles activity is still historically elevated. CDC reported 1,748 confirmed U.S. measles cases, across 33 jurisdictions and 19 outbreaks, with 94% outbreak-associated as of April 16. (CDC)

National influenza is declining and no new U.S. human H5 cases were reported this week. CDC FluView shows influenza activity continuing to fall overall, while CDC’s H5 monitoring reports no new human A(H5) infections this week and no evidence of person-to-person spread. (CDC)

**Preparedness implication:** For California agencies and health systems, the near-term priorities are MMR verification and rapid isolation pathways, mpox vaccine completion in higher-risk groups, completion of RSV infant protection before April 30, raw-dairy risk communication, and refreshed occupational IPC for wildlife/agricultural and frontline healthcare workers. (DHS Sacramento County)

## 2) California: Key Developments

### a) Respiratory viruses (influenza, RSV, SARS-CoV-2)

**What happened**  
California’s overall respiratory burden is currently modest. CDPH’s healthcare-professional hub says combined influenza/RSV/COVID-19 illness driving healthcare use is low, and Los Angeles County updated its respiratory surveillance dashboard on April 17 for the week ending April 11, indicating that local monitoring remains active.

**What we know**  
COVID-19 indicators remain low statewide, but RSV has lasted long enough for CDPH to extend RSV monoclonal antibody use through April 30.

**What we do not know**  
County-level heterogeneity may still matter, especially in pediatric settings, and changing COVID surveillance limits comparability with prior seasons.

**Public health response**  
Finish RSV protection for eligible infants and reinforce prompt testing/treatment for symptomatic high-risk patients.

**Practical implication**  
EDs, pediatric practices, birthing hospitals, and NICUs should use the lower overall respiratory burden as an opportunity to close remaining RSV immunization gaps rather than relax surveillance. (California Department of Public Health)

### b) Vaccine-preventable diseases (measles)

**What happened**  
Measles remains California’s clearest current outbreak-management challenge. CDPH reported 39 confirmed cases statewide as of April 13. San Francisco confirmed a case in an unvaccinated infant under 12 months, the city’s first case since 2019; the infant was exposed during international travel and recovered at home while SFDPH traced close contacts. Sacramento County’s April 6 health alert reported five county cases in 2026, including two recent cases with visits to a pediatric clinic, urgent care, and emergency departments before diagnosis; neighboring Placer County warned that additional linked cases were likely.

**What we know**  
Travel-associated importations and healthcare exposures are central.

**What we do not know**  
The full extent of downstream contact chains from early-April exposures.

**Public health response**  
Airborne isolation, rapid notification, specimen coordination, and aggressive contact tracing.

**Practical implication**  
Local health departments and clinicians should re-check MMR documentation, especially for healthcare workers, childcare staff, and travelers; triage protocols that identify fever-rash illness before rooming remain critical. (California Department of Public Health)

**County spotlight — Sacramento**  
The most policy-relevant lesson is occupational and facility exposure risk. Both recent Sacramento cases had multiple healthcare encounters before recognition, underscoring the need for front-desk screening, mask-first pathways, and immediate airborne precautions for suspected measles in outpatient and ED settings. (DHS Sacramento County)

### c) Foodborne/waterborne

**What happened**  
The most consequential recent California foodborne issue is the Raw Farm-associated STEC outbreak. CDC’s investigation remains open, and CDPH warned Californians not to eat recalled raw cheddar products.

**What we know**  
The outbreak involves raw cheese and earlier raw milk exposures, with severe outcomes including hospitalization and HUS.

**What we do not know**  
The true case count, since CDC notes many ill people recover without care or testing.

**Public health response**  
Recall enforcement, consumer advisories, and multistate case finding.

**Practical implication**  
Clinicians should ask specifically about raw dairy exposure when evaluating bloody diarrhea, avoid reflexive antibiotics when STEC is possible, and report suspect clusters promptly; local agencies should continue targeted messaging for households with young children. (CDC)

### d) Vector-borne/zoonotic

**What happened**  
Two California zoonotic signals stand out. First, mpox: CDPH says cases are rising statewide and San Francisco confirmed its first clade I mpox case in a resident. Second, avian influenza: CDPH continues to assess public risk as low but recently warned the public to avoid sick or dead marine mammals after H5N1 was confirmed in elephant seal pups at Año Nuevo.

**Who is affected**  
For mpox, current U.S. transmission remains concentrated in higher-risk sexual networks; for H5N1, occupational risk is highest among dairy, poultry, and wildlife-exposed workers.

**What we know**  
The SF clade I patient was unvaccinated, had international-travel contact, and was improving; CDPH says no person-to-person bird-flu spread has been detected in California.

**What remains uncertain**  
The extent to which clade I mpox will seed additional local transmission, and how wildlife events may alter exposure patterns for responders and coastal workers.

**Practical implication**  
Maintain mpox vaccine outreach now, before summer travel/events, and refresh PPE and exposure-reporting protocols for animal-contact occupations. (California Department of Public Health)

### e) Healthcare-associated infections & AMR

No major new California HAI/AMR emergency bulletin was prominent in the current 7–10 day window, but the operational picture still matters. CDPH continues to provide statewide HAI outbreak-response resources for local health departments and facilities, and its most recent hospital influenza-vaccination summary showed only about 72% of healthcare personnel in 402 hospitals vaccinated in 2024–25, down 2.7 percentage points from the prior year. For hospital epidemiology teams, that is a meaningful occupational-prevention signal ahead of the next respiratory season.

**Practical implication**  
Use the current lower respiratory burden to review fit-testing, measles readiness, and staff vaccination campaign design before fall. (California Department of Public Health)

## 3) USA and World: Key Developments Outside California

- **United States measles:** CDC reported 1,748 confirmed cases, 33 jurisdictions, and 19 outbreaks as of April 16; California is among affected jurisdictions. This remains the national development most likely to drive additional California importations and response costs. (CDC)
- **United States influenza:** CDC reports national influenza positivity continues to decline; Region 9 showed a localized increase likely related to fewer laboratories reporting, so Western trends should be interpreted cautiously. (CDC)
- **United States avian influenza:** CDC reported no new human A(H5) infections this week and no human-to-human spread, although sporadic infections continue to be linked to poultry and dairy exposures nationally. (CDC)
- **Multistate foodborne outbreak:** the Raw Farm E. coli O157:H7 investigation spans three states and remains open, reinforcing that California-origin products can generate interstate food-safety response demands. (CDC)
- **Global measles:** WHO reports a large, fast-moving Bangladesh outbreak, with 18,219 suspected cases, 2,897 lab-confirmed cases, and 164 suspected deaths reported between March 15 and April 14. For California, the relevance is travel-associated importation risk and the reminder that global measles control remains fragile. (World Health Organization)
- **Global mpox:** although older than this week’s window, WHO’s February rapid risk assessment still describes ongoing multi-clade mpox spread in diverse settings worldwide, including clade Ib circulation and new travel-linked detections in the Indian Ocean region; that remains operationally relevant to California travel medicine and vaccination messaging. (World Health Organization)

## 4) Watchlist (Next 2–4 weeks)

- Measles in California: because statewide case counts are already elevated and several recent events were travel-associated or healthcare-exposure related. (California Department of Public Health)
- Mpox in Bay Area and statewide networks: because California is seeing rising activity and San Francisco has now identified a clade I case. (California Department of Public Health)
- Infant RSV protection through end of April: because the prophylaxis window is still open and missed opportunities can still be corrected. (California Department of Public Health)
- Raw-dairy STEC: because the outbreak is still open and severe pediatric complications remain possible. (CDC)
- Avian influenza in wildlife/agricultural interfaces: because public risk is low, but occupational exposure persists and wildlife detections continue. (California Department of Public Health)
- Southern California vector season: flea-borne typhus reached a record 220 LA County cases in 2025, making vector-control readiness and clinician awareness worth monitoring as temperatures rise. (LACounty Public Health)

## 5) Data Notes & Caveats

CDC measles totals reflect confirmed cases reported to CDC as of noon Thursdays and may lag state dashboards. California respiratory interpretation is affected by surveillance changes, including new pediatric COVID-19 death reporting rules and broader dependence on hospitalization-based indicators. For foodborne outbreaks, reported case counts understate true burden because many ill people are not tested. California mpox dashboards also do not routinely specify clade and are refreshed less frequently than event-driven local alerts. (CDC)

## 6) One-Minute Takeaways

- Verify MMR immunity and exposure workflows in EDs, urgent care, pediatrics, and healthcare staff onboarding. (DHS Sacramento County)
- Push RSV infant immunization completion before April 30, especially in birthing and pediatric settings. (California Department of Public Health)
- Expand targeted mpox vaccine outreach now, with emphasis on completing the 2-dose series before summer travel and events. (San Francisco Media API)
- Reinforce raw dairy risk communication and rapid public-health notification for suspected STEC or HUS. (CDC)
- Refresh occupational IPC and PPE messaging for workers with animal exposure and for frontline clinicians evaluating measles-compatible illness. (California Department of Public Health)
