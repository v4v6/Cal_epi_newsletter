# Infectious Disease Epidemiology Brief

**Edition date:** May 27, 2026  
**Coverage window:** May 17–27, 2026, with older ongoing events included when they affect California preparedness, clinical recognition, occupational health, or response operations.

## 1) Executive Summary

Measles remains California’s leading vaccine-preventable signal: CDPH reported 49 confirmed state cases as of May 18; CDC reported 1,952 U.S. cases as of May 21, with 93% outbreak-associated. ([CDPH measles](https://www.cdph.ca.gov/Programs/CID/DCDC/Pages/Immunization/measles.aspx), [CDC measles](https://www.cdc.gov/measles/data-research/index.html))

Pertussis also requires school, childcare, prenatal, and healthcare attention: California reported 471 Q1 2026 cases, and Pasadena reported increased community activity with school-associated cases. (California Department of Public Health; Pasadena Public Health Department)

A California STEC O157:H7 outbreak linked to beef kofta at The Kebab Shop sickened nine residents; six were children, five were hospitalized, and two developed HUS. Sales were paused May 18; USDA-FSIS issued a public health alert because the product was no longer available for recall. ([CDPH, May 22](https://www.cdph.ca.gov/Programs/OPA/Pages/NR26-022.aspx))

Respiratory virus activity is low nationally, but California facilities should maintain cluster reporting, testing, and influenza A subtyping readiness. ([CDC respiratory viruses](https://www.cdc.gov/respiratory-viruses/data/index.html))

West Nile virus season is beginning: no California human cases were reported as of May 22, but dead birds and mosquito pools were positive. ([California West Nile Virus Website](https://westnile.ca.gov/))

Occupational H5N1 remains a One Health priority. California’s last confirmed human H5 case was January 14, 2025, but CDC described possible cat-to-human H5N1 transmission in a Los Angeles County veterinary professional. ([CDPH bird flu](https://www.cdph.ca.gov/Programs/CID/DCDC/Pages/Bird-Flu.aspx), [CDC bird flu](https://www.cdc.gov/bird-flu/situation-summary/index.html))

## 2) California: Key Developments

### a) Respiratory viruses

CDC’s May 22 update described very low acute respiratory illness healthcare-seeking, with COVID-19, RSV, and seasonal influenza activity low in most areas. California’s key action is readiness rather than surge response: report clusters, keep isolation pathways functional, and subtype influenza A when illness is severe, unusual, occupationally linked, or associated with animal exposure. ([CDC respiratory viruses](https://www.cdc.gov/respiratory-viruses/data/index.html))

### b) Vaccine-preventable diseases

**Measles:** California reported 49 confirmed 2026 cases as of May 18. Nationally, CDC reported 1,952 confirmed cases as of May 21 across 40 jurisdictions and nine international visitors, with 29 outbreaks in 2026. CDC also notes declining kindergarten MMR coverage, from 95.2% in 2019–20 to 92.5% in 2024–25, leaving approximately 286,000 kindergartners at risk. Implications: EDs, urgent care, schools, occupational health, and IP teams should ensure rapid masking/isolation, airborne precautions, exposure-list generation, and MMR verification for staff and high-risk contacts. ([CDPH measles](https://www.cdph.ca.gov/Programs/CID/DCDC/Pages/Immunization/measles.aspx), [CDC measles](https://www.cdc.gov/measles/data-research/index.html))

**Pertussis:** CDPH reported 471 California cases during January–March 2026. Pasadena’s May 11 advisory described significant community activity and directed clinicians to consider pertussis in prolonged cough, paroxysms, inspiratory whoop, post-tussive vomiting, or known exposure; Pasadena also reported school-associated clusters. Reinforce Tdap in pregnancy, prompt testing/treatment, exclusion guidance, and post-exposure management for infants, pregnant people, and healthcare workers. (California Department of Public Health; Pasadena Public Health Department)

### c) Foodborne and waterborne diseases

**STEC O157:H7, beef kofta:** CDPH is investigating illness linked to beef kofta served at The Kebab Shop. Illness onsets were March 27–April 30; nine California residents were affected, including six children, five hospitalizations, and two HUS cases; no deaths were reported. The chain paused beef kofta sales May 18, and FSIS issued a public health alert because the product was no longer available. Clinical implications: obtain stool testing for bloody diarrhea or severe abdominal cramps after exposure, monitor children for HUS, avoid empiric antibiotics/antimotility agents unless specialist guidance supports use, and ensure Shiga toxin testing plus culture/WGS forwarding. ([CDPH, May 22](https://www.cdph.ca.gov/Programs/OPA/Pages/NR26-022.aspx))

A prior Raw Farm raw-dairy E. coli O157:H7 investigation remains preparedness-relevant and reinforces recurring raw-dairy risk communication needs. ([CDC raw dairy outbreak](https://www.cdc.gov/ecoli/outbreaks/rawcheese-03-26/index.html))

### d) Vector-borne, zoonotic, and occupational infections

**West Nile virus:** California reported 0 human cases, 7 positive dead birds, and 5 positive mosquito samples as of May 22, including first county indicators for Sacramento and San Bernardino. Vector control should intensify surveillance/source reduction; clinicians should consider WNV neuroinvasive disease in compatible meningitis/encephalitis. ([California West Nile Virus Website](https://westnile.ca.gov/))

**H5N1/avian influenza:** CDPH lists 38 confirmed California human H5 cases, last reported January 14, 2025, and one additional probable case. Nationally, CDC reports 71 U.S. human A(H5) cases since February 2024. Risk remains concentrated in dairy, poultry, slaughter, wildlife, animal-control, veterinary, shelter, zoo, and laboratory settings. ([CDPH bird flu](https://www.cdph.ca.gov/Programs/CID/DCDC/Pages/Bird-Flu.aspx), [CDC bird flu](https://www.cdc.gov/bird-flu/situation-summary/index.html))

**Hantavirus:** No confirmed U.S. cases linked to the cruise outbreak were reported by CDC as of May 19, but ECDC reported 13 cruise-associated cases, including three deaths, as of May 26. Clinicians should ask about rodent exposure and travel for compatible febrile respiratory illness. ([CDPH Andes update](https://www.cdph.ca.gov/Programs/OPA/Pages/NR26-020.aspx), [SFDPH health alerts](https://www.sf.gov/resource/2024/health-alerts))

**Mpox:** CDPH’s mpox dashboard was refreshed May 22, but clade is not specified. California has had clade I activity, including San Francisco’s first clade I case confirmed April 14; CDPH reported it as California’s seventh identified clade I case since November 2024. CDC expects additional travel-associated cases. ([CDPH mpox](https://www.cdph.ca.gov/Programs/OPA/Pages/NR26-015.aspx))

### e) Healthcare-associated infections and antimicrobial resistance

No newly reported named California hospital, dialysis, or long-term-care outbreak was identified, but *Candida auris* and CRO/CRE readiness remain active priorities. Hospitals, SNFs, LTACHs, ventilator-capable facilities, and dialysis centers should maintain risk screening, interfacility transfer communication, contact precautions, cleaning verification, and reporting/isolate submission pathways. ([CDPH C. auris](https://www.cdph.ca.gov/Programs/CHCQ/HAI/Pages/Candida-auris.aspx))

## 3) USA and World: Key Developments Outside California

- **U.S. measles:** Nearly half of last year’s annual national total has already been reached by late May; travel, undervaccinated communities, and school/camp exposures are key California importation risks. ([CDC measles](https://www.cdc.gov/measles/data-research/index.html))
- **Animal-associated Salmonella:** CDC reports multistate outbreaks linked to backyard poultry and veiled chameleons, relevant to pediatric exposure history and household animal-contact counseling. ([CDC outbreaks](https://www.cdc.gov/outbreaks/index.html))
- **Global travel notices:** CDC lists Bundibugyo virus disease, chikungunya, Andes virus, and dengue notices; screen travel histories and apply biosafety/notification pathways when clinically indicated. ([CDC travel notices](https://wwwnc.cdc.gov/travel/notices), [SFDPH health alerts](https://www.sf.gov/resource/2024/health-alerts))

## 4) Watchlist: Next 2–4 Weeks

- **Measles:** summer travel, camps, and healthcare exposures; verify MMR status and airborne isolation workflows.
- **Pertussis:** school clusters and infant risk; monitor prenatal Tdap and healthcare worker exposure management.
- **STEC O157:H7:** follow-up case finding, HUS monitoring, and food traceback closure.
- **WNV and dengue:** early California arboviral season; mosquito surveillance and travel-associated dengue diagnostics.
- **H5N1:** dairy, poultry, wildlife, veterinary, shelter, animal-control, and laboratory occupational risk.
- **C. auris/CROs:** interfacility spread risk across high-acuity care, SNFs, LTACHs, and dialysis.

## 5) Data Notes & Caveats

Surveillance is lagged and uneven: CDC, CDPH, county dashboards, FDA CORE, and local advisories update on different schedules. Confirmed, probable, suspected, exposed, and outbreak-associated counts are not interchangeable; states may have more current local measles data. ([CDC measles](https://www.cdc.gov/measles/data-research/index.html))

Mandatory negative check: this sweep found active or relevant signals for measles, pertussis, respiratory viruses, avian influenza, Andes hantavirus, mpox, WNV, foodborne outbreaks, HAI/AMR, and travel-associated outbreaks. No credible new California signal was found for Sin Nombre virus/HPS, locally acquired dengue in 2026, mumps, rubella, polio, Hib, meningococcal disease, Legionella, Vibrio/shellfish outbreaks, or a named new healthcare-facility C. difficile/CRO/C. auris outbreak; absence here means no credible public source was identified, not absence of disease.

## 6) One-Minute Takeaways

- **Clinical recognition:** isolate and test febrile rash illness rapidly; ask about travel, vaccination, and exposure settings.
- **Public health preparedness:** keep measles, pertussis, STEC, and VHF/hantavirus contact-tracing templates ready.
- **Occupational health:** reinforce H5N1 PPE for veterinary, dairy, poultry, wildlife, shelter, and animal-control workers.
- **Infection prevention:** review *C. auris*/CRO screening and interfacility transfer communication this week.
- **Laboratories:** ensure reflex influenza A subtyping, STEC culture/WGS workflows, and consultation pathways for suspected clade I mpox or high-consequence viral infections.
